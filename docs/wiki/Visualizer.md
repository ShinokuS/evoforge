# Z-level Visualizer and Procedural Landscape

The EvoForge visualizer is a **debug observer of the real simulation**, not a second world model and not an owner of simulation state.

Its presentation contract is **full top-down with a geometry-derived horizontal Z cut**. Landscape art is generated by EvoForge itself at startup instead of depending on an external tileset.

## Module boundary

```text
simulation  pure Java, authoritative and headless
    ↑
core        libGDX presentation/debug visualizer
    ↑
lwjgl3      desktop launcher
```

`core` depends on `simulation`. Simulation contains no pixels, palettes, cutaway rules or camera visibility state.

`SimulationAssembly.start()` returns a runtime whose visualizer-facing capabilities remain read-only:

```text
SimulationView
SimulationTime
SimulationStepper
```

`SimulationView` now also exposes `TerrainExtentLookup`, a generic read-only `minZ/maxZ` fact about currently present terrain. This is simulation data, not presentation policy: it lets consumers know where terrain actually ends without guessing an arbitrary world height.

## Selected Z is a horizontal cut

Visualizer Z is the **standing/navigation plane**. Under the current Shape support law, terrain supporting standing position `(x,y,z)` is anchored at `(x,y,z-1)`.

For every visible XY, `LandscapeSliceResolver` resolves the cut in this order:

```text
terrain at selected Z       -> SOLID_BODY
terrain at selected Z - 1   -> CURRENT_SURFACE
open column below           -> nearest LOWER_SURFACE within debug depth
otherwise                   -> EMPTY
```

A mountain therefore does not disappear when the selected plane moves below its top. Its terrain cells intersect the cut and become solid cut material.

Lower terrain is visible only through genuinely open volume. A nearer solid/opaque cell closes the view to deeper surfaces.

## Visibility is geometric, not tied to absolute Z

The renderer has no rule such as `Z < 0 means cave` or `Z > 0 means outside`.

Instead it separates **what is drawn** from **the geometric context in which it is drawn**.

Resolved cells carry presentation measurements:

```text
bodyDepth         consecutive solid terrain above a cut-body cell
dropDepth         distance down to a visible lower surface
ceilingDistance   open vertical height before the nearest opaque cover
coverDepth        thickness of the first consecutive opaque cover
exposureDistance  open-volume distance to sky-connected exterior air
```

These measurements are independent. A tall cavern may have a distant ceiling while still being deep inside a mountain. A deep shaft may have a large drop while remaining directly open to sky.

### Asymmetric depth language

Looking downward from an elevation is not treated like being buried under material.

- **drop depth** progressively fades toward the background: nearby lower terrain remains readable while very deep isolated cells stop competing with the active slice;
- **cover/body depth** uses stronger progressive darkening;
- both preserve the same terrain color family instead of switching to a separate cave/cut hue;
- sufficiently deep regions can approach the background and lose fine texture without becoming a different simulation material.

This is a presentation cue, not a lighting simulation.

## Open-volume exposure

`VisibilityVolumeLookup` is the presentation-side geometric contract consumed by the cutaway resolver:

```text
solid(x,y,z)
opaque(x,y,z)
min/max occupied Z
```

The current adapter, `TerrainVisibilityVolume`, treats authoritative terrain as solid and opaque.

`LandscapeSliceResolver.Analysis` builds one bounded camera-local exposure field. It seeds air that is genuinely above the top opaque volume of its column, then performs a 6-neighbour flood/BFS through open volume.

The resulting shortest open-space distance means:

- an exposed field has exposure distance `0`;
- a cave mouth becomes exposed from the side without punching a fake hole through its roof;
- cells deeper inside the cave become progressively darker;
- a sealed chamber has no exterior path and saturates at the configured visual horizon;
- a real vertical opening exposes the cavern beneath it;
- large vertical caverns work because ceiling distance is measured independently from horizontal exposure.

The current visual exposure horizon is `12` cells. Beyond it, darkness saturates. This does **not** mean the cave stops existing or that simulation visibility is limited to 12 cells; it only prevents an unbounded presentation flood and gives deep interiors a stable visual floor.

## Future objects, roofs and terrain materials

The cutaway algorithm deliberately does not branch on names such as `mountain`, `cave`, `wall` or `house`.

A future structure/object can contribute to the same volume contract:

```text
terrain
future walls / roofs / large objects
        ↓
presentation volume adapter/composition
        ↓
VisibilityVolumeLookup
        ↓
unchanged cutaway resolver
```

Tests already inject a non-terrain opaque/solid contributor and verify that it blocks a lower sight line without becoming terrain.

This keeps the first implementation extensible without prematurely adding a simulation-wide Visibility subsystem. If future gameplay consumers such as actor line-of-sight, projectiles, lighting, smoke or glass need authoritative opacity/transmission, that requirement should introduce the simulation capability for those consumers. Camera cutaway exposure is **not actor perception**.

Binary `solid/opaque` is sufficient today. Partial transmission for glass, water, smoke or foliage remains a future extension point rather than a speculative API now.

## Mountains, caves and shafts

Mountains, cave rooms and shafts are ordinary geometry under this model.

A cave in a mountain is built from:

```text
solid terrain body around it
open cells for mouth/chamber
real lower terrain as floor
real terrain above as roof/cover
```

From a higher slice, the roof/body remains visible; the interior is not magically illuminated through it. At the cave slice, the side mouth provides exterior exposure and the chamber darkens with real air-path distance.

A cave under flat terrain uses the same rules. The acceptance world contains a separate walled cavern with a flat cap and one real vertical opening. The opening exposes the volume below because the geometry is actually open there, not because the renderer recognizes a cave special case.

The deep shaft likewise consists of genuinely absent intermediate terrain cells with a floor several Z levels below.

## Procedural visual language

```text
SimulationView
    ↓
TerrainVisibilityVolume
    ↓
LandscapeSliceResolver.Analysis
    ↓ role + depth/exposure context
LandscapeRenderer
    ├── surface/ramp -> ProceduralLandscapePack
    └── solid cut    -> ProceduralSliceArt
```

`LandscapeTopology` supplies normalized 8-neighbour masks and deterministic XYZ variants.

### Native profile

```text
logical simulation cell = 1 x 1
native procedural cell   = 16 x 16 pixels
```

Generated textures use `Nearest` filtering and duplicate padding. No source PNG, JSON descriptor, TexturePacker step or third-party asset installation is required.

### Surface art

The surface pack owns grass, edge/corner variation and all four cardinal Ramp orientations. Visual variants are deterministic by XYZ, so detail never flickers between frames.

### Solid cut art

`ProceduralSliceArt` uses a restrained shaded earth/vegetation-adjacent palette rather than a separate dirty-black material language. Body depth progressively removes brightness and detail, while the first few cut layers remain visually related to the surface above them.

### Ramp consistency

There is only one procedural Ramp visual language.

The same generated Ramp region is used whenever the Ramp is visible, including when its terrain cell intersects a lower horizontal cut. The renderer changes only environmental depth tint; it no longer substitutes a special ramp-cut sprite or creates an upper descent marker.

Authoritative Ramp semantics remain exclusively in `RampShape` and Navigation.

## Active standing-Z perimeter

The currently selected standing surface receives a presentation-only perimeter cue so the eye does not have to infer the active Z from shading alone.

Only the **outer cardinal boundary** of `CURRENT_SURFACE` is outlined. Adjacent cells that are both on the active surface produce no internal line, so this is not another grid.

The cue uses a pale low-saturation rim on the active side plus a narrow dark line just outside it. Both widths are derived from camera world-units-per-screen-pixel, so the contour remains approximately one screen pixel across zoom levels.

`LandscapeSliceResolver.isCurrentSurface(...)` owns the cheap structural query used by the overlay. The contour does not rebuild exposure BFS and introduces no new simulation state or terrain type.

## Controls and inspector

```text
Space          run / pause
N              one simulation tick while paused
PageUp/Down    move horizontal standing-Z cut
WASD           pan camera
mouse wheel    zoom
G              grid: off / subtle / debug
F2             Navigation transition overlay
F3             Ramp direction diagnostics
F4             lower visibility depth: 0 / 1 / 4 / 8
```

Click interaction always addresses `(x,y,selectedZ)`. Seeing a lower floor through a shaft does not silently change input Z.

The cell inspector reports the slice role plus geometric context: body depth, drop depth, ceiling distance, cover depth and exposure distance where relevant.

Objects remain intentionally primitive current-Z debug markers. Their future art/occlusion behavior should consume the same presentation context rather than teach the terrain resolver about object definitions.

## Z / cave torture world

`VisualizerDemoWorld` deliberately combines cases that stress the contract:

- broad lower meadow;
- irregular base plateau on standing `Z=1`;
- four real cardinal base Ramps;
- stacked mountain and successive local Ramps through standing `Z=4`;
- mountain cave with a side mouth, chamber, real floor and real roof;
- separate cavern beneath a flat cap;
- real vertical opening into that cavern;
- high cliff without a graphics-only `CliffShape`;
- deep open shaft whose floor is several elevations below;
- slow/fast movers preserving authoritative 8-tick / 2-tick Movement behavior.

Headless tests cover terrain extent lifecycle, horizontal-cut priority, the cheap current-surface query used by the perimeter, body/drop/cover/ceiling measurements, side-mouth exposure distance, sealed chambers, tall caverns, future non-terrain occluders, Ramp topology and Movement timing.

Manual acceptance should move through the relevant Z levels and verify that structure remains understandable without complete transparent upper floors.

## Performance boundary

Exposure is computed once per rendered camera range, not independently for every tile. The BFS is deliberately bounded by the visual exposure horizon and the requested lower-depth range.

The active-Z perimeter uses direct current-surface membership checks only; it does not duplicate the exposure analysis.

Current terrain extent is maintained incrementally by `TerrainSystem`; the visualizer does not invent an arbitrary maximum Z.

The current adapter may scan the global terrain Z extent to find the top opaque cell for a column. If representative deep/sparse worlds show this to be expensive, optimize behind the existing volume boundary with a per-column top query or cached presentation data. Do not change visibility semantics merely to optimize representation.

## Deferred presentation decisions

Still intentionally deferred:

- explicit adjacent-layer X-ray/build mode;
- real roofs/structures/large objects contributing to the volume once those consumers exist;
- partial optical transmission for glass/water/smoke/foliage;
- authoritative actor LOS and lighting systems;
- additional procedural materials and material transitions;
- alternate visual styles and hand-authored packs;
- larger sprites for creatures, trees, equipment and buildings;
- richer shadows/compositing;
- generated-atlas export tooling;
- dirty/chunk visual caches before profiling proves need.

## Next simulation consumers

The visualizer remains non-authoritative while simulation proceeds toward:

```text
Occupancy
    ↓
Pathfinder
    ↓
observable action outcome
    ↓
first agent / Cow vertical slice
```

Those systems may gain diagnostics, but renderer visibility never determines simulation truth.
